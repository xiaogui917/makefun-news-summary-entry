World news — Wednesday, 11 November 2026

Spain stopped work in its first general strike in 14 years. Millions joined a 24-hour stoppage by the CCOO and UGT unions over housing and pay, under the slogan "wages, housing, prices". Transport and schools were disrupted before a 29 November election; a poll put strikers at 23% of workers. Spain is 750,000 homes short, its central bank says. (Al Jazeera)

US prosecutors charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot to drive fuel-laden vans at RAF Fairford, a US-used base in England. The indictment, unsealed on Tuesday in Manhattan, links them to nearly 20 attacks in Europe and the US. (BBC)

US-mediated peace talks on Ukraine entered a second day in Abu Dhabi. Envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner mediated between Rustem Umerov and Vladimir Medinsky; day one ran about four hours and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called it "constructive", with no breakthrough expected. (BBC)

The House voted 211-204 to send impeachment articles against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, blocking Republican Thomas Massie's bid over the Iran war. (BBC) In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom led Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 2,600 votes, 97% counted, in one of two uncalled Senate races; no Democrat has won a Senate seat there since 1982. (AP)

Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000 cases, with 5,387 dead, the second-deadliest on record. (Reuters) World leaders began two days of COP31 talks in Antalya, Turkey. (AFP) A Florida judge let President Donald Trump's $10 billion BBC defamation case proceed, dismissing his trade-practices claim. (Reuters)

Asian shares fell after hot US October inflation sent Wall Street lower, and oil held near its lowest since August after Mr Trump said on Monday the war with Iran was "effectively over". (Bloomberg) Britain, Canada and Australia marked Remembrance Day with two minutes' silence at 11 a.m.
